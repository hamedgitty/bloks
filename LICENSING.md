# Licensing, in plain English

Bloks is under the [Functional Source License, Version 1.1, MIT Future
License](LICENSE). The full text is short and worth reading, but here is
what it means in practice.

This page is a summary written to be helpful, not a legal document. Where
the two disagree, [LICENSE](LICENSE) is what counts.

## What you can do

Everything you would expect from an open source project, with one
exception:

- **Use it at work.** Internal use is explicitly permitted, at any size
  of company, with no fee and no seat count.
- **Read, fork, patch and redistribute it.** The source is public and
  stays public.
- **Build it yourself.** Running `pnpm build` and using the result is
  free, forever, including commercially inside your own organisation.
- **Use it while doing paid work for someone else**, as long as they are
  also using Bloks under this licence.
- **Teach with it and research with it**, non-commercially.

## The one thing you cannot do

You cannot **sell Bloks, or something substantially like it, to other
people.** Rebranding it and putting it on a pricing page is the case this
licence exists to prevent.

That is the whole restriction. If you are not selling a competing
product, this licence behaves like MIT for you.

## It becomes MIT on a timer

Every version of Bloks converts to the plain MIT licence **two years
after it is released**. That conversion is irrevocable and automatic;
nobody has to agree to it at the time, and it cannot be withdrawn.

So the code you are reading today is open source with a two year delay,
not open source with a catch.

## Why not just MIT

Bloks is meant to become a product that funds its own development. MIT
would let anyone take the work, put a price on it, and compete using it
without contributing anything back. That is a real risk for a small
project and a fatal one for a solo maintainer.

FSL is the narrowest restriction that prevents exactly that and nothing
else. It was written by Sentry for the same reason, and it is used by
projects that wanted to stay genuinely open without being trivially
resellable.

## What this means if you contribute

By opening a pull request you agree that your contribution is licensed
under the same terms as the rest of the project, which includes the
future MIT grant. There is no contributor licence agreement to sign and
no copyright assignment.

## Not sure whether your use is allowed

It almost certainly is. If you genuinely are not sure, open a discussion
and ask. A clear yes in writing costs nothing and is better for both of
us than a guess.
